The dock opens at six. Sam checks the lock and writes the time on the sheet. Two trucks were already in the yard, both from the north route. The first driver had the blue invoice. The second had a copy that did not match the pallet count, so Sam held that truck and called the office.

We'll sort the short pallets before lunch. It's easier when the aisle is empty. Don't stack the glass crates. They crack if the forklift turns too fast, and last month we lost a whole row that way. The replacement glass arrives Friday.

The budget sheet sits on the small desk (the one by the window). Bring both vendor quotes to the ten o'clock meeting. If the quotes are still over the limit, we will ask each vendor for a second round and decide by Friday. Lunch is not provided. People can use the cafe on the corner.

The night crew mopped the bay. The floor is dry now. Mark the wet sign anyway when it rains, because the ramp stays slick for an hour. A spare mop hangs behind the door. Use it. Then put it back.

June will log the damaged boxes in the red book. She counts them twice. The first count is a guess from the doorway. The second count is the one we keep. If the two counts disagree, she counts a third time and writes all three numbers.

The small room on the third floor is free after two. The big room is taken by the safety talk. Sam will book the small room and send the note before noon. If the projector fails again, we will print the page and read it. That happened in March. It was slow, and it worked.

Keep the radio on channel two. Channel one is the yard. Channel two is the office. A long call about a missing pallet can wait until the trucks are unloaded. Write the pallet id, the bay, and the time. Leave the story for the end of the shift.

The side door sticks in cold weather. Lift the handle, then push. Don't kick it. The hinge was replaced in August and the paint is still fresh. Tell June if it sticks again. She has the work order form in the top drawer.

Park the spare forklift by the east wall. The charger cord stays coiled on its hook beside the aisle. Keys go in the box when the shift ends. The last person out turns off the radio and writes the time. That line is how we know the bay was closed.

It's not just about the price, it's about the date. It doesn't just list the line, it checks the total. Not only the blue invoice but also the pallet count has to match before the truck leaves.
